A tree is appropriate for the doctor structure because it is hierarchical. Therefore, we can have doctors and show their subordinates.
This could also display knowledge and specialization so we can ensure patients are given to doctors who accurately understand their condition
and can give them the best care possible. Those with more serious and urgent cases should be given to the best doctors available to ensure
that they are given the best care possible. 

Preorder traversals are useful for copying or saving a tree structure in order to use it later or to understand how a tree is structured.
Inorder is used to search through trees or retrieve sorted data to use in other functions or mathematical expressions. Postorder is useful
when deleting from trees, so child items are visited first and can be deleted. They are also useful for bottom-up calculations. Each one
of these traversal methods has many different uses and offers many different ways to interact with trees to make them useful.

Heaps help simulate a real-world emergency system by automatically sorting patients based on need for care, so that the most urgent are taken first.
They also make it easy to add to, remove, and peek at to see who is next in line. If you were to use a regular list, it would be time and space 
consuming to sort manually or by iterating through the list. A heap has an insertion and extraction time of O(log n) so the time is close to 
consistent and time stays close to the same even with a large system.
